FOREWORD 
 
I am delighted to present the twenty -fifth annual report of the Karnataka Electricity Regulatory 
Commission for the year 2023-2024, prepared in accordance with Section 105 of the Electricity Act, 
2003. 
This year signifies the twenty -fifth anniversary of regulatory oversight in Karnataka. The Commission 
has dedicated itself to fostering an efficient, economical, and competitive power sector, ensuring 
a balance between the interests of all stakeholde rs and protecting the legitimate rights of 
consumers in the state. 
The report includes a summary of the Commission’s annual budget and a ctual expenditure for the 
year 2023-24. It also details the activities of the Commission during the year 2023-2024 and plans for 
the upcoming FY25. 
In this report, we have made a sincere effort to highlight the Commission’s work in fulfilling its duties 
under the Electricity Act, 2003. 
The Commission has issued Tariff Orders for the Transmission licensee KPTCL and five Dist ribution 
licensees: BESCOM, MESCOM, CESC, HESCOM, and GESCOM. It also includes one Rural Electric Co-
operative Society (HRECS) and two Special Economic Zones (SEZs), namely MSEZ and AEQUS SEZ, 
for the financial year 2023-24. During this financial year, the Annual Performance Review of all these 
entities was also conducted. Tariff Orders were issued for the Annual Performance Review for FY22 
and FY23, the Annual Revenue Requirement for FY24 and FY25, and the revision of Retail Supply Tariff 
for FY24 and FY25 for ESCOMs and other Distribution Licensees  on 12th May, 2023 and 28 th February 
2024 respectively. 
Additionally, during the financial year, the Commission notified the  Regulations and resolved 
petitions arising from disputes among licensees and generators , as well as between licensees and 
open access consumers. 
I would like to take this opportunity to reassure all stakeholders of the Commission’s continued efforts 
to make the sector competitive, financially viable, consumer-friendly, and safe as envisioned in the 
Electricity Act, 2003. The Members of the Commission have been cooperative in discharging the 
functions of the Commission. The Officers, Consultants, and members of the Staff have also provided 
valuable assistance. I would also like to acknowledge the valuable advice received from Members 
of the Advisory Committee, Licensees, and Stakeholders.

2.    INTRODUCTION 
 
The Karnataka Electricity Regulatory Commission (KERC) was established in 1999 as per 
the provisions of the Karnataka Electricity Reform Act, 1999 and began functioning 
from 15th November, 1999. Consequent to the enactment of the Electricity Act, 2003 
on 10th June, 2003, the KERC continued to function under the provisions of this Act. 
As per Section 105(1) of the Electricity Act, 2003, the KERC is required to prepare an 
Annual Report, giving a summary of its activities during the previous year and the 
copies of the Report are to be forwarded to the State Government. Further, as per 
Section 105(2) of the Electricity Act, 2003, the copies of Annual Reports shall be laid 
before the State Legislature.   
 
This is the Twenty -Fifth Annual Report of the Commission, which includes summary of 
the activities of the Commissio n during the financial y ear 2023-24 and the proposed 
program for the financial year ahead i.e. 202 4-25. It also contains the Bu dget for the 
financial year 2024-25 and the actual expenditure for the financial year 2023-24.  
 
During the financial year 202 3-24, the Commission  has completed the proceedings 
relating to disposal of Tariff petition s and has issued the Tariff Orders on the An nual 
Performance Review for FY22 & FY23 and the ARR for FY24 to FY25 and Revision of 
Retail Supply Tariff for FY24 and FY25 on 12th May, 2023 and 28th February, 2024 
respectively.  The revised tariff is effective for the electricity consumed from the first 
meter reading date falling on or after 01.04.2023 and 01.04.2024 as per the Tariff Orders 
dated12th May, 2023 and 28th February, 2024 respectively. 
 
The Commission has issued Regulations, Amendments to the Regulations, Generic Tariff 
Orders for Solar, Wind, Waste to Energy, Mini Hydel, Co -generation, Bio- Mass power 
projects, discussed in the subsequent chapters. 
 
The Commission has disposed of 140 Petitions arising out of disputes among licensees 
and generators as well as licensees and open access consumers during the year. The 
Summary of the Commission’s activities is discussed in the subsequent chapters.

3.2 FUNCTIONS OF THE COMMISSION  
 
(1) As per Section 86 of the Electricity Act, 2003, the State Electricity 
Regulatory Commissions are required to discharge the following functions:   
(a) Determine the tariff for generation, supply, transmission and 
wheeling of electricity, wholesale, bulk or retail, as the case 
may be, within the State;   
(b) Regulate electricity purchase and procurement process of 
distribution licensees including the price at which electricity shall be 
procured from the generating companies or licensees or from other 
sources through agreements for purchase of power for distrib ution 
and supply within the State;  
(c)  Facilitate intra-state transmission and wheeling of electricity;  
(d) Issue licences to persons seeking to act as transmission licensees, 
distribution licensees and electricity traders with respect to their 
operations within the State;  
(e) Promote cogeneration and generation of electricity from 
renewable sources of energy by providing suitable measures for 
connectivity with the grid and sale of electricity to any person, and 
also specify, for purchase of electricity from su ch sources, a 
percentage of the total consumption of electricity in the area of a 
distribution licensee;  

[PDF page 9]
9 
 
(f) Adjudicate upon the disputes between the licensees, and 
generating companies and to refer any dispute for arbitration;  
(g) Levy fee for the purposes of the Electricity Act;  
(h) Specify State Grid Code consistent with the Grid Code specified 
under clause (h) of sub-section (1) of section 79;  
(i) Specify or enforce standards with respect to quality, continuity and 
reliability of service by licensees;  
(j) Fix the trad ing margin in the intra -State trading of electricity, if 
considered necessary; and  
(k) Discharge such other functions as may be assigned to it under this 
Act.  
(2) The State Commission is also required to advise the State Government on 
all or any of the following matters, namely: -  
 
(i) promotion of competition, efficiency and economy in activities of 
the electricity industry;  
(ii) promotion of investment in electricity industry;  
(iii) reorganization and restructuring of electricity industry in the State;  
(iv) matters concerning generation, transmission, distribution and 
trading of electricity or any other matter referred to the State 
Commission by the State Government.  
The State Commission is required to ensure transparency while exercising its 
powers and discharging its functions.  
In discharge of its functions, the Commission is being guided by the National 
Electricity Policy, National Electricity Plan and Tariff Policy published under 
Section 3 of the Electricity Act, 2003.

4. THE COMMISSION’s OFFICE & FUNCTIONS OF THE SECRETARY: 
 
4.1 THE COMMISSION’s OFFICE  
The Chairman of the Commission is its Chief Executive as per Section 84 of the 
Electricity Act, 2003.  The Commission is supported by Secretariat, headed by the 
Secretary. 
 
Besides, the Commission’s Office has senior level officers on contract, serving as 
Director (Tariff), Director (Technical), Consultant (Tariff), Consultant (Tech) and 
Consultant (Legal). Further, there are Deputy Directors both on its rolls and on 
deputation, with requisite expertise in specific areas of Transmission, Distribution, 
Tariff, etc., to assist the Commission. 
 
The details of the Officers and members of Staff working in the Commission are 
mentioned in this report. The Organizational Chart of the Commission is annexed 
as Annexure – I. 
 
4.2 FUNCTIONS OF THE SECRETARY: 
The functions of the Secretary are as follows:  
 
(a) To supervise and guide the functioning of all the Officers and Staff of the 
Commission;  
 
(b) To prepare or ensure preparation of  brief notes and summaries of all 
pleadings presented by various parties in each case before the Commission 
in discharge of its functions; 
 
(c) To assist the Commission in the proceedings relating to the powers 
exercisable by the Commission;  
 
(d) To ensure execution of the Orders passed by the Commission; 
 
(e) To collect from the State Government or other offices, companies or firms 
or any other party as may be directed by the Commission, such information 
as may be considered useful for the purpose of efficient discharge of the 
functions of the Commission under the Act and place the said information 
before the Commission; and  
 
(f) To keep in his custody, the seal and records of the Commission.

9.5   TRANSMISSION AND DISTRIBUTION LOSSES: 
 
 
The Karnataka Power Transmission Corporation Ltd., is the State Transmission utility 
vested with the function of transmitting power from the generating stations to the 
interface points with the ESCOMs. The transmission loss is due to the loss of energy 
for technical reasons incurred in the transmission system.  In order to contain these 
losses, the KPTCL is augmenting existing stations / lines besides adding new                 
stations / lines in the transmission system.  The KPTCL had reported transmission 
loss of 2.977% for the FY24.

The ESCOMs enter into power purchase agreements with generators and the 
same is accounted as power purchase quantum in million units, at the 
generation bus of the generating stations. This energy is transmitted through the 
transmission system of the KPTCL to the interface points with the ESCOMs. The 
difference of the energy at generation bus and energy at the interface points 
with ESCOMs is reckoned as t ransmission loss. The ESCOMs distribute the energy 
through their distribution network to cater to the needs of different category of 
consumers in their areas of operation. The difference of energy recorded at the 
interface points and the sum of the energy recorded at the consumer level is 
reckoned as distribution loss. The losses in the distribution system are due to 
technical and commercial reasons like errors in meters, meter reading, billing, 
theft / pilferage of electricity, etc.

9.8 ADDITIONAL SURCHARGE FOR FY24 AND FY25: 
 
a) Additional Surcharge for FY24 
 
The Commission in its Tariff Order-2023 had determined a common additional 
surcharge of 148 paise per unit for FY24, applicable to all ESCOMs and no 
concession was extended to renewable sources.  
  
Some of the stakeholders challenged the above order in the  Hon’ble High 
Court of Karnataka in WP No. 10401/2023 and other connected petitions. The 
Court passed orders and the relevant portion of the Order dated 19.06.2023 
of the Hon’ble High Court is reproduced below:  
 
“5. In view of the aforesaid facts and circ umstances and the material on 
record, which indicates that the impugned order has been passed in violative 
of principles of natural justice coupled with the aforesaid memo filed on behalf 
of the KERC, without expressing any opinion on the merits/demerits of the rival 
contentions, I deem it just and appropriate to set aside the impugned order 
and remit the matter back to the KERC insofar as it relates to levying of 
additional surcharge of Rs. 1.48 per unit applicable to all open access 
transactions for recon sideration afresh in accordance with law after 
providing/furnishing the data submitted by the PCKL to the petitioner and 
others and also provide them an opportunity and hearing in this regard. 6. It is 

[PDF page 25]
25 
 
also relevant to state that on 30.05.2023 while staying the impugned order, this 
Court directed the petitioners to continue to pay surcharge at the rate of 35 
paise per unit and the said interim order remains in force even till today. Under 
these circumstances, though the 2nd respondent – BESCOM has raised bi lls 
applying the rates as per the impugned order, in view of the present order, 
whereby the impugned order is set aside, I further direct the petitioners to 
continue to pay the additional surcharge at the rate of 35 paise till the 1st 
respondent-KERC passe s appropriate orders in accordance with law. It is 
needless to state that the said payment by the petitioner at 35 paise per unit

business models, allowing sale of energy under net/ gross metering at

3. The abov e SRTPV arrangements recognize only the sale of energy 
between consumers and the distribution utility. However, of late a new 
concept of sale of energy between consumers to consumers has 
emerged. The sale of surplus power by the consumer/ prosumer to another 
consumer at an agreed tariff is known as Peer to Peer (P2P) solar energy 
transactions, through Block  Chain based platform, which is gaining 
popularity. The concept is seen as a promising innovation in the energy 
sector for Decentralization, Renewable Energy Integration, Efficiency and 
Flexibility, Empowering Prosumers, Cost Savings, Grid Resilience, 
Environmental Impact, Innovation in Technology, Community 
Engagement and Regulatory Evolution. The P2P concept would help in 
recovery of investment much fast er as compared with the existing 
arrangements. 
4. The objective of framing the Regulations is to promote rooftop solar, 
efficient utilization of   existing assets and to implement innovative 
technologies by facilitating transactions of rooftop solar energy th rough 
blockchain based P2P platform besides ensuring reasonable returns on 
the investments. 
5. The P2P solar transactions offer various benefits, the success of which 
depends on regulatory support, technological advancements, and 
widespread adoption within co mmunities. As the energy sector evolves, 
P2P transactions are likely to play a significant role in shaping the future of 
sustainable energy systems.

PRUDENCE CHECK:  
The Commission has been conducting the Prudence Check of the Capital 
Expenditure of the Transmission and Distribution Licensees once in two years, as per 
its earlier Guidelines. Accordingly, the Commission had entrusted the work of 
conducting prudence chec k of capital expenditure for FY21 and FY22 to 
Consultants during FY23, as a part of exercise for Annual Performance Review of 
KPTCL, ESCOMs and HRECS. 
 
After analyzing the replies on the findings of the Consultant on the imprudent works 
and justifications furnished by the licensees, the Commission has been disallowing 
the amount of interest and depreciation on the works which were found to be not 
meeting the norms of Prudence, in the revised ARR during the Annual Performance 
Review of the respective years, wherever necessary.

11. ADVISORY COMMITTEE OF THE COMMISSION: 
 
In accordance with Section 34 of the Karnataka Electricity Reform Act, 1999 and 
Section 87 of  the Electricity Act, 2003, a State Level Advisory Committee has been 
constituted by the Commission to advise on: 
(i) Policy matters; 
(ii) Matters relating to quality, continuity and extent of services provided by the 
     Licensees; 
(iii) Compliance by the Licensees with the conditions and requirement of their 
     Licence; 
(iv) Protection of consumers’ interest; and 
(v) Electricity supply and overall Standards of Performance by utilities; 
       During the year, two meetings of the Advisory Committee were held. 
a. The proceedings of the 68 th Advisory Committee Meeting held on 30.01.2024 
reiterated as under:  
Summary of the Meeting on Power Supply and Safety Measures: 
1. Hydro Generation Shortfall: 
Shortfall Details: PCKL reported a hydro generation shortfall of approximately 
3500 MU for FY24, with 1600 MU already accounted for. To conserve hydro 
generation, 17 MU energy is being purchased under Section 11, which will only 
be available until the second week of February 2024. 
Future Challenges: Meeting demand in March and April 2024 will be difficult 
with available energy sources. KPCL thermal units need to operate at a higher 
Plant Load Factor (PLF) of 80-85%. 
2. Energy Procurement and Coal Supply: 
Procurement Challenges: PCKL’s attempts to procure energy through DEEP 
portal tenders resulted in high bids (Rs. 8/unit for RTC power and Rs. 13/unit for 
peak power), with insufficient availability in the market. 
Coal Supply: Currently, coal availability for KPCL stations is not an issue, but 
there are concerns about potential constraints d uring the summer due to the 
upcoming general election. Efforts are ongoing to ensure continuous coal 
supply. 
 

[PDF page 58]
58 
 
3. Ensuring Continuous Power Supply: 
Priority During Elections: Continuous power supply needs to be ensured, 
particularly to industries, urban areas, and during exams for students. 
Load Shedding: In case of severe shortages, power supply to agricultural pump 
sets may be limited to 5 hours in a staggered manner. 
4. Capacity Addition and Renewable Energy (RE) PPAs: 
Future Demand:  Demand is expected to  double in the next seven years, 
requiring an addition of 1000 MW capacity annually, preferably from firm RE 
sources. 
RE PPA Renewals: Steps are being taken to renew expiring RE PPAs and retain 
them to meet Renewable Purchase Obligation (RPO) targets. 
5. Transmission System Constraints: 
Upgrades and Issues:  Transmission system upgrades are underway, but 
evacuation problems due to Right of Way (RoW) issues persist. The policy for 
compensating landowners might be adopted to ease RoW issues. 
6. Maintenance and New Projects: 
Maintenance: Ensuring maintenance of KPCL stations to minimize outages, 
especially before elections. 
New Projects: Exploring options for new thermal power plants near coastal 
regions based on imported coal and potential run-of-river generating stations. 
7. Installation of ABT Meters: 
Progress: Installation of ABT meters and EMS systems is progressing, with 
completion expected by March 2024. 
8. Pumped Storage Projects: 
Conversion Cost: The conversion cost for pumped storage projects is estimated 
at Rs. 3.50-4.00 per unit. 
9. Safety Audit and Accident Prevention: 
Safety Audits: Safety audits are being conducted, with discrepancies being 
rectified regularly. Safety awareness programs are also being implemented. 
Accident Reduction Measures: Safety equipment is being provided to field staff, 
with training and surprise inspections to ensure compliance with safety 
guidelines. 
 

[PDF page 59]
59 
 
10. Addressing Illegal Connections and Load Shedding: 
Illegal Connections: Efforts to regularize illegal pump sets and re-conductor old 
lines to reduce accidents and improve safety. 
Unscheduled Load Shedding: Addressing unscheduled load shedding in rural 
areas and ensuring continuous power supply to IP sets. 
11. Collaboration and Regulations: 
Stakeholder Collaboration: FKC CI expressed interest in collaborating on 
technical safety audits. Concerns about non -implementation of regulations 
were raised. 
Unauthorized Buildings: Addressing unauthorized buildings flouting electrical 
safety norms and issues related to RR numbers for mixed-load buildings. 
The Commission emphasized the need for proactive measures to ensure continuous 
power supply, enhance safety, and address future demand challenges.

amending the Regulations and also in the determination of tariff.

ANNUAL REPORT 2024-25
Chairperson's Statement
It is my privilege to present the Annual Report of the Competition Commission of India (CCI) for the
year 2024-25.
The Competition Act, 2002 ('the Act) mandates the establishment of a Commission to prevent anti-
competitive practices, promote and maintain market competition, protect consumer interests, and
ensure freedom of trade for all market participants, keeping in view the economic development of the
country.
On May 20, 2024, CCI completed fifteen years of its establishment since notification of the
substantive provisions of the Act relating to anti-competitive conduct. In these fifteen years, CCI has
been consistently working towards prevention of anti-competitive practices, ensuring fair
competition and providing a level playing field to all market players.
During the year 2024-25, CCI registered information with respect to 37 antitrust matters and received
139 merger filings. It passed final orders in 47 antitrust matters and found contravention in 04 such
cases. 138 merger cases were decided/ disposed of including 02 cases with remedies.
Since inception, 1311 antitrust cases have been registered and 1266 merger filings were received till
March 31,2025.
767 antitrust cases were closed at prima facie stage after consideration by the Commission. 415 cases
were closed after issuing order for investigation under Section 26(1) of the Act out of which 224 cases
were closed after investigation as no contravention was found and in 191 cases, the Commission
found contravention and passed orders under Section 27 of the Act. At the end of the year, remaining
123 cases were pending at various stages.
On combination side, 1246 merger filings were decided/disposed of and 20 cases were pending at the
year end.
Anti-Trust
During the period, the Commission inquired into cases relating to anti-competitive agreements
(including cartels & bid rigging) and abuse of dominant position across diverse sectors of the

The Commission vide its order dated November 11,2024 imposed a penalty of Rs. 213.14 crore on
Meta for abusing its dominant position through WhatsApp's 2021 Privacy Policy. The Commission
found that the Policy, introduced on a take-it-or-leave-it basis, compelled users to accept expanded
data collection and mandatory sharing of data with Meta group entities, without providing any opt-
out mechanism, thereby amounting to an imposition of unfair conditions on users. Further, the
Commission also held that the sharing of user data with other Meta entities for purposes unrelated to
the provision of WhatsApp services created entry barriers and denied market access to competitors in
the online advertising space, and that Meta had leveraged its position in the OTT messaging market to
protect its interests in the digital advertising market. Consequently, Meta was directed to cease such
practices and adopt specified behavioural remedies within a set timeframe.
The Commission also found the Table Tennis Federation of India (TTFI) and its affiliates engaged in
anti-competitive conduct in violation of Sections 3(4) and 4 of the Act. The Commission found that
the restrictive clauses in TTFI's Memorandum of Association and WhatsApp advisories limiting
player participation in events amounted to refusal to deal and abuse of dominance in the markets for
organising and participating in table tennis tournaments in India. Considering the corrective steps
already taken by TTFI, including withdrawal of such restrictions and amendments to its governing
documents, the Commission issued a cease-and-desist order but did not impose any monetary
penalty.
During the period, the Commission closed two cases against IREL (India) Ltd. The cases involved
information received regarding the mining and sale of Beach Sand Sillimanite, and Ilmenite in India,
respectively, wherein allegations were made against IREL for imposing excessive and discriminatory
pricing and supply conditions. It was alleged that IREL engaged in such practices due to its dominant
position in the market for the mining and sale of Beach Sand Sillimanite, and Ilmenite in India,
respectively, in potential violation of Section 4 of the Competition Act, 2002. However, the
Commission, vide order(s) dated October 08, 2024 and January 17, 2025 held that although IREL
holds a dominant position in the relevant market of "mining and supply of Beach Sand Sillimanite in
India," and "mining and supply of Beach Ilmenite in India"
, no abuse of its dominant position could
be established. Accordingly, no contravention of the provisions of Section 4 of the Act was found.
Combinations (Mergers & Acquisitions)
During 2024-25, the Commission received 139 combination notices in sectors such as Financial
Markets, Pharmaceuticals and Healthcare, Information Technology and Services, PVC & Chemicals,
Auto & Auto Components and Power & Power Generation. Further, a total of 22 notices were filed
through the Green Channel route during the said period.
Framing of Regulations pursuant to the Competition (Amendment) Act, 2023
On April 11, 2023 the Competition (Amendment) Act, 2023 was passed by the Central Government
incorporating certain new provisions in the Competition Act, 2002 and amending certain previously
existing ones. This created a need for amendment/ repeal/ overhauling of the various regulations
framed by the CCI as well as for introduction of certain new ones.

Accordingly, CCI framed various implementing regulations during the current Financial Year, after

(i) The Competition Commission of India (Combinations) Regulations, 2024
In view of the significant changes introduced by the Amendment Act, the CCI notified on 10th
September 2024 the Competition Commission of India (Combinations) Regulations, 2024,
which repealed and replaced the Competition Commission of India (Procedure in regard to the
transaction of business relating to combinations) Regulations, 2011. The new Combinations
Regulations, inter alia, provide for the following: (a) Value of transaction and criteria for
substantial business operations in India; (b) Form of notice for the proposed combination; (c)
Exercise of rights in case of open offer and acquisitions on stock exchanges; (d) Procedure for
filing notice and review of combinations; and (e) Procedure for modification to the proposed
combination.
(ii) The Competition Commission of India (General Regulations), 2024
The Competition Commission of India (General) Regulations, 2009 were repealed and
replaced by the Competition Commission of India (General) Regulations, 2024 consequent
upon the changes introduced through the Competition Amendment Act of 2023, to align the
General Regulations with the amendments made in the other Regulations and/ or framing of
new Regulations by the CCI, besides reviewing the existing Regulations based on experience
gained since 2009 till date.
(iii) The Competition Commission of India (Manner of Recovery of Monetary Penalty)
Regulations, 2025
Based on the experience gained during implementation of the Competition Commission of
India (Manner of Recovery of Monetary Penalty) Regulations, 2011 over the years and to
streamline the process of recovery, certain amendments were deemed fit to be incorporated in
the said Regulations. Accordingly, the Competition Commission of India (Manner of Recovery
of Monetary Penalty) Regulations, 2025 were framed to repeal and replace the existing
regulations.

Legal Developments
During the period, important legal issues were settled by various Judicial Fora in the field of
Competition Law Jurisprudence.
In a significant development, the Hon'ble Supreme Court has allowed the Transfer Petition filed by
the CCI under Article 139A of the Constitution of India, thereby clubbing together over 30 Writ
Petitions instituted by various sellers and mobile manufacturers before different High Courts.
The Hon'ble Court was pleased to accept the submissions made by the Learned Attorney General for
India, who appeared on behalf of the CCI, that the issues raised in the said Writ Petitions were
identical in nature. It was therefore submitted that, to prevent the possibility of conflicting decisions
in the matter and to ensure judicial consistency, it would be appropriate for a single High Court to
adjudicate all such matters collectively in accordance with law.
Finding merit in the submissions advanced by the Learned Attorney General, the Hon'ble Supreme
Court directed the transfer of all pending Writ Petitions on the subject from various High Courts to the
Hon'ble High Court of Karnataka. The Court further directed that any new Writ Petition filed on
similar grounds shall also be transferred to the Karnataka High Court for adjudication.
It may be noted that the Petitioners, comprising primarily of sellers and mobile manufacturers, were
aggrieved by the fact that they were impleaded by the Commission without having been explicitly
made Opposite Parties in the prima facie order passed by the Commission.
As regards the statutory appeals against CCI orders, during the reporting period, 17 appeals have been
decided by the Hon'ble National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT). The orders passed by

Section 27 of the Act, wherein penalties and remedies/cease and desist orders were passed; orders
passed under Section 26(2) of the Act and order passed under Section 31(1) of the Act. Majority of the

Market Studies
As part of its mandate to promote and sustain competition across sectors, the CCI initiated a Market
Study on the Diagnostic Medical Imaging (DMI) Equipment industry in India, with a specific focus
on CT scanners and MRI machines, in March 2023. The study aimed to assess the structure of the
industry, understand the level of competition across different nodes of the value chain, and identify
challenges faced by stakeholders. Key findings and observations of the study along with
recommendations, were published in July 2024 and the same is available on the website of the CCI.
The findings revealed that the DMI equipment market is largely import-driven and exhibits
oligopolistic characteristics, with significant market concentration among a few global original
equipment manufacturers. While the new equipment segment faces moderate to high entry barriers,
the refurbished equipment and after-sales service markets are relatively more fragmented and
dynamic. Based on these insights, the study recommended promoting domestic manufacturing
through phased capacity-building, encouraging transparency in pricing and spare part availability,
strengthening testing infrastructure, and enabling broader access to spare parts and services to foster a
more competitive and efficient market ecosystem.

A. INTRODUCTION
(1) Objectives
CCI was established in the year 2003 and got its enforcement and regulatory powers after the
substantive provisions of the Act relating to anti-trust enforcement and regulation of
combinations that came into force on May 20, 2009 and June 1, 2011, respectively.
The Competition Commission of India (CCI) is an expert body established by the Competition
Act 2002 ('The Act'). CCI has been established to prevent practices having adverse effect on
competition, to promote and sustain competition in markets, to protect the interests of
consumers and to ensure freedom of trade carried on by other participants in markets, in India,
and for matters connected therewith or incidental thereto. The Act prohibits anti-competitive
agreements (Section 3), abuse of dominant position (Section 4) and provides for regulation of
Combinations (Mergers and Acquisitions) (Sections 5 & 6).

(iii) The year in review
(a) Landmark decisions of the Commission:
1. CCI issues a cease-and-desist order to the Table Tennis Federation of India and its affiliate
bodies
The Competition Commission of India (CCI) passed an order dated 12.12.2024 under the
provisions of Section 27 of the Competition Act, 2002 (the Act) against The Suburban Table
Tennis Association (TSTTA), Maharashtra State Table Tennis Association (MSTTA), Gujarat
State Table Tennis Association (GSTTA) and Table Tennis Federation of India (TTFI), for
contravention of provisions of Sections 3(4) and 4 of the Act, resulting in refusal to deal and
abuse of dominance. The matter was initiated following a complaint by the TT Friendly Super
League Association under Section 19(1)(a) of the Act, which alleged that TTFI and its affiliates
engaged in anti-competitive conduct. This included restricting players' participation in events
through a WhatsApp message issued by the TSTTA General Secretary and enforcing restrictive
clauses in the TTFI's Memorandum of Association. The CCI determined that TTFI and its
affiliates held a dominantposition and restricted competition in the relevant markets for the
organisation of table tennis leagues/events/ tournaments and the provision of services by the
players for table tennis leagues/events/ tournaments, in India. Acknowledging corrective
measures taken by TTFI, such as withdrawing restrictive communications and amending
governing documents, the CCI issued a cease-and-desist order but refrained from imposing
monetary penalties.

2. CCI imposes a monetary penalty of Rs. 213.14 crore on Meta for anti-competitive
practices in relation to 2021 Privacy Policy Update
The Competition Commission of India (Commission) vide its order dated 18.11.2024 imposed
a penalty of Rs. 213.14 crore on Meta for abusing its dominant position. In this matter, the
Commission examined the manner in which WhatsApp's 2021 Privacy Policy was
implemented and how user data was collected and shared with other Meta companies. Starting
from January 2021, WhatsApp notified users about updates to its terms of service and privacy
policies. The in-app notification stated that, effective 08.02.2021, users were required to accept
these terms, including expanded scope of data collection as well as mandatory data sharing with
Meta companies, to continue using WhatsApp. The Commission concluded that the 2021
policy update by WhatsApp on a 'take-it-or-leave-it' basis constitutes an imposition of unfair
conditions under the Act, as it compels all users to accept expanded data collection terms and
sharing of data within Meta Group without any opt-out. Accordingly, the Commission found
that Meta (through WhatsApp) has contravened Section 4(2)(a)(i) of the Act. Further, as regards
sharing of data between Meta companies, the Commission has concluded that (a) sharing of
WhatsApp users' data between Meta companies for purposes other than providing WhatsApp
Service creates an entry barrier for the rivals of Meta and thus, results in denial of market access
in the display advertisement market, in contravention of the provisions of Section 4(2)(c) of the
Act; and (b) Meta has engaged in leveraging its dominant position in the OTT messaging apps
through smartphones to protect its position in the online display advertising market and the

The Competition Commission of India (Manner of Recovery of Monetary Penalty)
Regulations, 2025
The Competition Commission of India has notified the Competition Commission of India
(Manner of Recovery of Monetary Penalty) Regulations, 2025 repealing the Competition
Commission of India (Manner of Recovery of Monetary Penalty) Regulations, 2011. These
Regulations, inter-alia, envisage issuance of the demand notice along with the order passed by
the Commission for imposition of penalty under the provisions of the Competition Act, 2002.
The demand notice issued would provide a time period of not less than 60 (sixty) days from the
date of receipt of order of the Commission. The new Regulations also provide that if the amount
specified in the demand notice is not paid within the period specified the enterprise or the person
concerned would be liable to pay simple interest at the rate of one per cent for every month or
part of a month on the outstanding amount. Other amendments include definition of a'person in
default', in addition to an 'enterprise in default' requirement of legal heirs to pay the penalty to
the Commission if the person in default is deceased, simultaneous recovery of the amount of
penalty through attachment and sale of movable and immovable property if a person or
enterprise in default fails to pay the penalty on time as stated in the recovery certificate, in
accordance with the extant rules. These regulations also provide that if the income-tax authority
to which a reference has been made by the Commission initiates recovery proceedings, the
recovery proceedings initiated by the Commission shall stand sine die deferred.

(vii) Impact of Competition Act enforcement on Consumers and Economy
Competitive markets are the cornerstone of a thriving economy. The effective enforcement of
competition law plays a vital role in maintaining this competitiveness by safeguarding the
competitive process. By preserving this process, consumers are empowered with greater
choice, new businesses are encouraged to enter the market, and firms are driven to improve
efficiency and innovate to stay competitive. The core objective of competition law is to ensure
that companies compete based on efficacies, thereby delivering higher-quality goods and
services at more affordable prices. However, when competition is underminedwhether
through the abuse of dominance, collusive agreements among firms, or anti-competitive
mergers and acquisitions prices tend to rise, product quality may decline, and consumer
choice and innovation are likely to diminish. Through the enforcement of competition law,
competition authorities aim to detect, prevent, and address anti-competitive behaviour while
fostering a strong culture of competition throughout the economy.
In pursuit of this objective, the Competition Commission of India has, over the past year,
examined antitrust cases spanning various sectors of the economy. In instances where
violations of the Act were found, appropriate penalties were imposed, and necessary corrective
measures were directed. During the year, the Commission has decided various matters relating
to cartelization and anti-competitive conduct that impact consumers and the economy in
diverse sectors viz. Sports, Digital Communication, Liquor, Aviation, Infrastructure, Ports,
Public Procurement, Automobiles etc. The Commission has also reviewed mergers and
acquisitions in sectors such as entertainment, IT, construction materials, cell phone towers etc.
that have wider impact on the economy. With its orders cutting across various sectors of

The Commission inquires into any alleged contravention of the provisions of Sections 3 and 4

NCLAT upheld the order of CCI on merits and reduced the penalty amount in the matter
of Delicasy Continental Pvt Ltd Vs. Competition Commission of India (Suo Motu Case
No.01 of2020)
The matter arose out of a general complaint dated 07.08.2018 received by the Commission
alleging bid-rigging in tenders invited by the Department of Agriculture, Government of Uttar
Pradesh for soil sample testing. The complaint pertained to alleged bid rigging in respect of two
e-tenders. After an investigation by DG, CCI and hearing the parties, the Commission vide its
order dated 04.04.2022 held Opposite Parties (OPs) and their office bearers i.e. Yash Solutions,
M/s Satish Kumar Agarwal, M/s Siddhi Vinayak, M/s Saraswati Sales Corporation, Austere
System Pvt. Ltd., Delicasy Continental Pvt. Ltd, Fimo Infosolutions Private Limited, M/s
Toyfort and Chaitanya Business Outsourcing Pvt. Ltd. to be in contravention of Section 3 of the
Competition Act, 2002 (the Act). The Commission, in terms of Section 27 (a) of the Act,
directed the OPs and their respective office bearers to cease and desist from indulging in
practices found in contravention of the provisions of Section 3 of the Act and imposed the
penalty upon the OPs, under the provisions contained in Section 27(b) of the Act.As M/s Satish
Kumar Agarwal, M/s Siddhi Vinayak, M/s Saraswati Sales and M/s Toyfort are sole
proprietorship concerns. No separate penalty was imposed on the respective proprietors of
Aggrieved by the above-mentioned order under Section 27 of the Act in Suo Motu Case No. 01
of 2020 of the Commission Delicasy Continental Pvt. Ltd. filed an appeal before the Hon'ble
NCLAT under Section 53(B) of the Act. In this appeal, after conclusion of hearing, the Hon'ble
NCLAT vide its order dated 31.05.2024 in Competition Appeal (AT) No. 32 of 2022 held that,
"In the present case, the appellant was acting as a member of the cartel and was providing cover
bid for the successful bidder Austere Systems. In view of peculiar facts and circumstances of the
present case, where almost all bidders for soil testing are first time bidders and relevant
turnover of firms from the aforesaid business is NIL, the concept of relevant turnover in such
cases would not be correct, as it would lead to NIL penalty and allow the parties involved to go
scot-free. Hence, we agree with the Commission's approach of taking the total turnover for
computation and imposition of penalty. At the same time considering the fact that the Appellant
was in a supporting role in this cartel, by providing the cover bids, we are of the view that the
penalty in such cases should be less than from those in the main role. Based on the above
discussion, we hold (i) The order of the Commission in respect of holding the appellant guilty
under Section 3(3)(c) and 3(3) (d) read with section 3(1) and order passed under Section 27(a)
regarding cease-and desist order are upheld. (ii) The penalty under Section 27 (b) is reduced to
3% of average annual turnover for last 3 years, instead of 5% as imposed by the commission."
Hence, the Hon'ble NCLAT upheld the Commission's order dated 04.04.2022 on merits with
some modifications in the quantum of penalty imposed on Delicasy Continental Pvt. Ltd.

of Section 3 of the Act for indulging in cartelisation. The Commission, in terms of Section 27 (a)
of the Act, directed the OPs and their respective Directors to cease and desist from indulging in
practices found in contravention of the provisions of the Act and further under the provisions
contained in Section 27(b) of the Act, imposed the penalty upon the OPs and their office bearers.
Although, OP-2 and its office bearers were granted 100% reduction in the penalty amount under
Lesser Penalty provisions. Aggrieved by the above-mentioned final order of the Commission
under Section 27 in aforementioned Suo Motu Case No. 03 of 2017, OP-3 i.e. Godrej & Boyce
Manufacturing Co. Ltd. and its office bearers filed appeals i.e. Competition Appeal (AT) No.
18-19 of 2019, titled Godrej & Boyce Manufacturing Co Ltd v. Competition Commission of
India & Ors. and Rajiv Jhangiani & Ors. v. Competition Commission of India before the
Hon'ble NCLAT under Section 53(B) of the Act. In these appeals, after the conclusion of
hearing, the Hon'ble NCLAT vide its order dated 05.04.2024 held that, "...from the facts one
thing is clear the dry cell battery market is an oligopoly with three predominant players i.e.
Eveready Industries India Ltd, Indo National Ltd and Panasonic which form a primary cartel
members, controlling the market with combined market share of 88% as against miniscule 2%
market share of appellant. Admittedly, the turnover of the appellant qua dry cell battery was
lesser than M/s Geep and rather it suffered losses in this business, though Geep was in profits in
later years. Both the appellant company and M/s Geep were in similar position and asking M/s
Geep to pay 1% of the turnover as a penalty and the present appellant to pay 4% to our mind did
not synchronize. Nevertheless, we are aware of the fact despite charging 4% as penalty, the
amount of levy in terms of rupee(s) is far less than 1% penalty amount paid by M/s Geep but we
cannot ignore Geep was in profits in later years. Thus, in view of the above we hereby decrease
the penalty imposed upon the appellant to 2% of the turn-over, while maintaining the penalty
imposedupon its officials. However, we are not inclined to waive interest as pendency in appeal
and continuation of stay would not be a ground for waiver. The penalty reduced is considering
the peculiar facts of this case and be not treated as a precedent." The Hon'ble NCLAT upheld
the Commission's order on merits with some modifications in the quantum of penalty imposed
on Godrej & Boyce Manufacturing Co. Ltd. while maintaining the penalty imposed upon its
officials. Further, the Hon'ble NCLAT held that the penalty reduced in this matter is considering
the peculiar facts of this case and not to be treated as a precedent.

F. MATTERS RECEIVED REGARDING COMBINATION
The provisions relating to combinations came into force on Ist June 2011. Through the Competition
(Amendment) Act, 2023, the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) notified amendments to the
Competition Act, 2002 (Act) in respect of the regulatory framework for mergers and acquisitions,
including those on deal-value thresholds. The MCA also introduced the rules for Criteria for
Exemption of Combinations, Criteria of Combinations (Green Channel) and Minimum Value of
Assets or Turnover. Further, the CCI has repealed the existing combinations regulations and
introduced a revised set of comprehensive new combinations regulations (Combinations Regulations
2024). The amendments in the Act relating to combinations, aforesaid rules, and Combinations
regulations came into effect from 10" September 2024.
The Act defines combination to mean acquisition of one or more enterprises by one or more persons or
merger or amalgamation of enterprises, whose value of asset or turnover or value of transaction meet
the threshold specified under the Act. The Act prohibits any combination which causes or is likely to
cause an appreciable adverse effect on competition (AAEC) within the relevant market in India. In
case, AAEC can be addressed through suitable modifications, the Commission may accept and/or
propose suitable modifications to the parties. Parties proposing to enter into a combination shall give
notice of such combination in the prescribed form along with the requisite fee to the Commission to
enable the Commission to assess the proposed combination from perspective of competition. Under
section 20(1) of the Act, the Commission may, upon its own knowledge or on receiving information
relating to a combination inquire into whether such combination has caused or is likely to cause an
appreciable adverse effect on competition in India (Suo moto cases).
The Green Channel scheme, introduced in August 2019 (through amendment in Combinations
regulations), provides for granting automatic approval to certain combinations. These were
incorporated in Regulation 5A of the CCI (Procedure in regard to the transaction of Business relating
Combinations) Regulations, 2011. After the provisions of Section 7 of the Competition
(Amendment) Act, 2023 coming into effect, provisions relating to the Green Channel are given in
Section 6(4) and 6(5) of the Act read with the Competition (Criteria of Combination) Rules, 2024
(Green Channel Rules). Earlier these were incorporated in Regulation 5A of the Combination
Regulations 2011.The number of Green Channel cases filed during the FY 2022-23, 2023-24 and

G. REFERENCES BY CENTRAL
GOVERNMENT/STATE GOVERNMENTS
The State intervenes in the market either by enacting legislations to prescribe rules of the game or by
formulating policies relating to trade, commerce, industry, business, investment, disinvestment,
taxation, IPR, procurement, etc. These interventions usually strengthen the invisible hands of the
market and seek to promote competition. However, despite best intentions and exercise of the best
skills, care and due diligence, some interventions by the State may inadvertently restrict the ability of
economic agents to effectively compete in the marketplace.
Section 49 of the Act enables the Central Government and as well as State Governments, while
formulating a policy on competition, including review of laws related to competition or any other
matter, to make a reference to the Commission for its opinion on the possible effects of such policy on
competition. On receipt of such a reference, the Commission shall, within 60 days of making such
reference, give its opinion to the Central Government or the State Government, as the case may be.
Under Section 54 of the Act, the Central Government may, by notification, exempt (a) any class of
enterprises if such exemption is necessary in the interest of security of the State or public interest; (b)
any practice or agreement arising out of and in accordance with any obligation assumed by India
under any treaty, agreement or convention with any other country or countries; (c) any enterprise
which performs a sovereign function on behalf of the Central Government or a State Government,
from the application of the Act, or any provision thereof, and for such period as it may specify in such
notification.
In terms of Section 55 of the Act, the Commission shall, in exercise of its powers or the performance
of its functions under the Act, be bound by such directions on questions of policy as the Central
Government may give in writing to it from time to time.

H. REFERENCES FROM AND TO
STATUTORY AUTHORITY
CCI has been mandated, inter alia, to promote and sustain competition in markets in India. In some of
the markets, a sectoral regulator may also be present. In such a scenario, situations may arise wherein
a sectoral regulator, while pursuing its mandate, comes up with a measure/decision which may have
competition concerns. Similarly, the Commission, while promoting competition in markets, may
come up with measure(s)/ decisions which may have sectoral concerns. A measure or a decision may
not always promote the objectives of the sectoral regulator and the Commission simultaneously. In
recognition of this, the Act provides for mutual consultation between CCI and the sectoral regulators.
Where in the course of a proceeding before any statutory authority, an issue is raised by any party that
any decision which such a statutory authority has taken or proposes to take is or could be contrary to
any of the provisions of the Act, then such a statutory authority may make a reference with respect to
such an issue to the Commission. It may also make a reference on its own. On receipt of such a
reference under Section 21 of the Act, the Commission is obliged to give its opinion within 60 days of
receipt of such reference to such a statutory authority, which shall consider the opinion of the
Commission. Section 21A of the Act also provides for similar reference from the Commission to any
statutory authority.

I. COMPETITION ADVOCACY
Enforcement and advocacy are twin arms of the competition law having supplemental and
complemental role towards building competition compliant culture in the country. Section 49 (3) of
the Competition Act, 2002 (Act) mandates the Commission to take suitable measures for promotion
of competition advocacy or culture, creating awareness and imparting training about competition
Issues.
The Commission in pursuance of the advocacy mandate engages with stakeholders such as judiciary,
industry, academia, central & state governments, central and state public sector undertakings (PSUs),
professional bodies, trade associations, training academies etc.
Competition advocacy is an outreach process that influences the economic behaviour of enterprises,
elicits support for the economic principles of competition and convinces stakeholders about the
innate advantages of competition law. The statutory mandate for competition advocacy reinforces the
view that enforcement alone cannot achieve all the intended goals of competition law. There are
visible as well as hidden market distortions and anomalies in the economy which require
implementation of enforcement and advocacy both as a combined tool for the correction of the
markets.

• 10" National Conference on Economics of Competition Law- 16 March 2025
The Competition Commission of India (CCI) organized the 10th edition of its annual
National Conference on Economics of Competition Law on Sunday, March 16, 2025 at
Gulmohar Hall, India Habitat Centre, New Delhi. The Conference, which brings together
scholars, practitioners, and experts working in the area of economics of competition law, is
being organised by the CCI every year since 2016.
Shri Harsh Malhotra, Hon'ble Minister of State in the Ministry of Corporate Affairs; and
Minister of State in the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways was the Keynote
Speaker at the Conference. Smt. Ravneet Kaur, Chairperson, CCI, delivered the Special
Address at the Inaugural Session and Shri Inder Pal Singh Bindra, Secretary, CCI gave the
welcome remarks. Shri Anil Agrawal, Member, CCI extended the vote of thanks.
The Conference comprised a Plenary and two Technical sessions on "Digital Dynamics:
Markets, Competition & Innovation'; and 'Exploring Mergers: Structure,
Competition and Synergy' where researchers presented papers on economics of
competition law. The first session chaired by Dr. Nishant Chadha, Director, Policy and
Research, Indian School of Business, Hyderabad. The second session was chaired by Dr.
Uday Bhanu Sinha, Professor, Delhi School of Economics. Dr. Mansi Kedia, Senior
Fellow, ICRIER and Dr. Saish Nevrekar, Assistant Professor, Ashoka University were
Discussants in the two Technical Sessions.
The Conference Plenary was on 'Settlement and Commitment: A New Era of Trust-
Based Fast-Track Market Correction'. The Plenary was moderated by Shri Prasanto
Kumar Roy, Senior Adviser, FTI Consulting. Sh. Kamlesh Chandra Varshney, Member,
SEBI; Dr. Harsha Vardhana Singh, Former Deputy Director General, WTO, Chairman,
IKDHVAJ Advisers LLP ; Ms. Jyoti Jindgar Bhanot, Adviser (Economics), CCI; Dr.
Navneet Sharma, Advisor, Government and Policy, Helicon Consulting; Ms. Manjushree
RM, Senior Resident Fellow, Vidhi Centre for Legal Policy were distinguished panellists
in the plenary.
The Conference was attended by approximately 200 attendees from various fields viz.
Economists, Academicians, Scholars, Lawyers, Practitioners, Competition agency
officials and Students etc.

nominate SRPs from the Officers who have recently retired from the post not below
the rank of Joint Secretary (or equivalent) to the State Government. The main focus of
this scheme is to impart training on competition issues to state government officials in
general and procurement officials in particular. Each Resource Person nominated
under the scheme is required to organise and conduct two advocacy programs every
month and carry out incidental activities related to organising the advocacy programs
with government departments and PSUs. An SRP is also encouraged to identify policy
documents/bills/legislations/Act for detailed analysis from competition law
perspective with the help of the Competition Assessment Toolkit. They are expected
to undertake the main task of conducting advocacy events besides other ancillary
works of 'Tenders Study and Recommendation'; and 'Study of the policy
documents/Bills'.

countries. The objective of the study is to explore, analyse, and potentially address any
issues or challenges associated with ensuring fair competition in the sector.
(iii) Memberships of international organizations:
i. International Competition Network
The ICN is a virtual international organization and its members represent competition
authorities from across the globe. The ICN platform allows for a dynamic dialogue on
competition policy principles within the global antitrust community. CCI is a member of
the ICN Steering Group (2023-25) and is also co-chair of the ICN's Operational
Framework Working Group (OFWG) along with the Competition Bureau, Canada.
As OFWG Co-Chair, CCI has been involved in the review of the ICN's Operational
Framework based on discussions with the OFWG members, in accordance with core
principles of the ICN.

M. CAPACITY BUILDING
CCI has been conceived as an expert statutory body, administering and enforcing the Competition
Act, 2002 which requires knowledge and skills traversing more than one discipline. A
multidisciplinary approach is needed for understanding concepts used in the Act and framing
regulations under it. Further, constant economic and market developments in Indian as well as world
economies necessitate corresponding capacity building of employees of CCI. Therefore, skill
enhancement and regular updation of knowledge are essential for smooth functioning of a
multidisciplinary organization like CCI. This is achieved through capacity building programmes
such as trainings/workshops/lectures etc. These programmes are conducted with the help of experts
from overseas multilateral agencies and competition authorities as well as by domestic experts and
organizations specializing in the field of law, economics, finance, management, etc. In addition to
that, CCI conducts in-house trainings and Peer-to-Peer sessions wherein inter-divisional sharing of
knowledge and information takes place.
CCI regularly invites eminent personalities from various fields as speakers to address its employees
under Distinguished Visitors Knowledge Sharing Series (DVKS) and Special Lecture Series (SLS).
